## The problem it solves

Every system an organisation adds asks the same two questions: who is this, and what are they allowed to do. Answered separately each time, the answers drift. Access lingers after someone leaves. Permissions granted for one project outlive it. An audit request that should take an afternoon takes three weeks, because the answer has to be assembled from eight places.

## How Qeet approaches it

Identity is solved once and used everywhere. A person signs in a single time and reaches everything they are entitled to reach. Access is granted and revoked in one place, and the record of who did what is kept where it can actually be produced.

The practical test is simple: when someone joins, the right things open on their first day, and when they leave, they close on their last — everywhere, at once, without anyone filing a ticket.

## Why it constrains everything else

Identity is the only capability in this portfolio that every other product depends on. That makes it the highest-consequence component Qeet operates, and it is treated accordingly: changes to it have the widest reach of anything the organisation ships.

It is also what makes the rest possible. Intelligence that respects permissions, notifications that reach the right person, an audit trail that spans products — none of it works unless the identity underneath is shared.
