## What it does

Qeetrix holds the decisions that every Qeet product would otherwise make separately: what a button looks like, how far apart things sit, which colours mean what, how an interface behaves for someone using a keyboard or a screen reader.

Those decisions are made once, tested once, and inherited by every product. A change to how something works propagates outward rather than needing to be re-litigated in eight places.

## Why it matters

Consistency across a portfolio is usually described as a visual nicety. It is not. It is a compounding cost.

When each product decides for itself, accessibility is done well in some places and forgotten in others, an interface pattern a customer learns in one product does not transfer to the next, and every team spends a portion of its time rebuilding things that already exist. A shared foundation removes that tax permanently, and it is the reason a portfolio this size can move at all.

## Where it fits

Qeetrix and Qeet ID are the two things everything else depends on. Between them they answer "who is this person" and "what should this look like" — the two questions no product can avoid and none should answer alone.
