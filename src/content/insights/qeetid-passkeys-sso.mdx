When Qeet ID launched, we said the roadmap would lead with what organisations ask for first rather than what is quickest to build. Three things came up in every conversation. All three are now live.

## Sign-in without passwords

People can now sign in with the same mechanism they use to unlock their phone or laptop. There is no password to choose, to forget, to reuse across sites, or to be tricked into typing into a convincing copy of your login page.

That last point is the one worth dwelling on. The most common way organisations are broken into is not a clever technical attack — it is someone being persuaded to enter their credentials somewhere they should not. Passwordless sign-in does not make that attack harder. It makes it structurally impossible, because there is no credential to hand over.

## The accounts people already have

Most people arriving at a new tool already have a work account somewhere. Qeet ID now lets them use it, which removes a step at exactly the moment a new user is deciding whether this is going to be tedious.

## Directory integration

Larger organisations do not want to manage accounts twice. They want their existing employee directory to be the source of truth: when someone joins, they get access; when someone leaves, it is gone, everywhere, without anyone filing a ticket.

Qeet ID now connects to those directories. Access follows employment automatically.

## What this adds up to

None of these is a headline feature in isolation. Together they are the difference between a product an engineering team can adopt and a product an organisation can adopt — and the gap between those two is where most identity tools stop.
